## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Does it look AI-generated? Mostly no — it reads as a disciplined product UI, with one loud exception.**

**LLM assessment (A):** The core vocabulary is trustworthy — one card shape, one button family, the account-circle reused across four screens, a coherent monochrome SVG set, sentence-case headings, purposeful sub-250ms motion, `prefers-reduced-motion` honored, a 430px column with safe-area insets. Above the slop line. But three tells would make a fluent user pause: (1) **a banned decorative purple gradient** on `.lisa-card` (line 224); (2) **inconsistent vocabulary** — LISA allowance is a loud purple card on the detail screen but a calm white card on the Forecast tab; (3) **emoji leaked back in** — `✓` on nine toasts and raw `↑/↓` in transaction discs, the exact emoji-as-personality the design system says was removed. Clean on: side-stripe borders, gradient text, hero-metric cliché, card-grid soup, container overflow.

**Deterministic scan (B):** detect.mjs exit 2, 3 warnings — `single-font` (line 38), `dark-glow` (line 93), `layout-transition` (line ~239).
- `single-font` → **false positive for this register.** Product UI intentionally uses one system family (our DESIGN.md mandates it); a display/body pairing would be wrong here.
- `dark-glow` → **false positive.** This is a light-mode app; the flagged glow is the sanctioned blue `+` FAB (DESIGN.md §4 FAB glow), not a dark-mode aesthetic.
- `layout-transition` → **minor, low-confidence.** A `transition` touching a layout property on a progress bar; use `transform` instead. Low impact.
- Notably the detector **missed** the real issue (the purple gradient card) because its gradient rule targets gradient *text*, not gradient card fills.

**Visual overlays:** none — no browser-automation tool is exposed in this environment, so no user-visible overlay was produced.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely good, trustworthy money tool — the undo-everywhere safety net and the honest, self-limiting forecast copy are best-in-class for the category, and the calm single-number-first layout delivers the "Quiet Ledger" thesis on the primary screens. The single biggest opportunity: **the app doesn't yet live up to its own design system in two places** (the purple LISA card and the split allowance/import treatments), and its most trust-critical moment — entering a passphrase to import shared data — is its least-designed surface.

## What's Working

1. **Undo-everywhere + backup-before-import** (lines 1158–1174, 1360–1372, 1399–1407): every destructive path snapshots with a 6s Undo, and import auto-exports a backup first. DESIGN.md principle 4 made real — exemplary for a stressful domain.
2. **Honest forecast framing** (lines 877, 974): "Simple estimate… not financial advice. It doesn't predict one-off lump sums." Refuses false precision and names its own limits — the quiet-utility register done right.
3. **Disciplined core vocabulary**: account-circle, one card, one blue accent, sentence-case headings, monochrome SVG set applied uniformly across Home/Accounts/detail/Forecast — the "earned familiarity" bar is met on primary surfaces.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The purple gradient LISA card breaks the design system and the brand promise.**
- Why it matters: `.lisa-card` (line 224, used at 821) is a saturated purple `linear-gradient` with translucent-white bars — a second accent hue + a bright gradient, violating the One-Blue Rule, the "no bright gradients" anti-reference, and the deprecated-`--lisa`-token note. It's the loudest surface in a "Quiet Ledger" and reads as the gamified fintech the product defined itself against — and it's inconsistent with the calm white ISA card on the Forecast tab.
- Fix: replace with the same white card + hairline + blue progress bar used for ISA allowance (line 938). Keep the data; drop the purple.
- Command: `/impeccable quieter`

**[P1] Keyboard / screen-reader access: tap-only `div onclick` rows.**
- Why it matters: 8 `.tx` and `.mi` rows (lines ~240, 800) are `<div onclick>` — not focusable, not announced as buttons. The entire transaction-edit and account-menu flow is unreachable by keyboard or screen reader, and PRODUCT.md explicitly commits to WCAG AA. Sam (a11y persona) is blocked. Low-opacity labels on the purple card (`.75/.65`) are also borderline for 4.5:1.
- Fix: make interactive rows real `<button>`/`role=button` + `tabindex=0` with Enter/Space handlers and visible focus rings; recheck contrast once the purple card is gone.
- Command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P1] Consistency: two allowance treatments, two import flows, a native `alert()`.**
- Why it matters: LISA rendered two ways; two separate import entry points (`importData` JSON restore line 1387 vs `importPrompt` encrypted bundle line 1015) invite the wrong choice; `alert('Please enter an account name')` (1216) sits amid custom modals. Inconsistent vocabulary is the core "subtly-off" signal and will confuse even the owner.
- Fix: unify LISA/ISA into one card; label the two imports distinctly ("Restore JSON backup" vs "Import shared sync file") or merge them; replace `alert()` with inline `.fi` validation.
- Command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] Add Account modal exposes 7 fields at once (>4 rule).**
- Why it matters: name/type/bank/balance/rate/deposit(/contrib) all at once (326–361) — the highest-friction moment for the self-serve stranger, at the exact point of first commitment; violates "calm over comprehensive."
- Fix: lead with essentials (name, type, balance); fold rate/deposit/contrib behind an "Add forecast details (optional)" disclosure.
- Command: `/impeccable distill`

**[P3] The passphrase-import moment lacks reassurance, and small emoji/polish regressions.**
- Why it matters: `#pass-bar` (1434) names no sender/date/file contents and wrong-pass is a bare red line — the app's emotional valley and trust-defining interaction for the partner persona. Separately: `✓` on nine toasts + raw `↑/↓` tx icons reintroduce emoji; Home shows "Total savings £0.00" above the empty state; the toast is `white-space:nowrap` (line 263) so long messages overflow the 430px column; `.ft th` uses a small uppercase eyebrow (line 233).
- Fix: show file metadata before decrypt + a recovery hint on failure; drop `✓` from toast copy; suppress the total until ≥1 account; allow toast wrapping; sentence-case the forecast header.
- Command: `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan / the self-serve stranger (PRODUCT.md 12–15):** sees "Total savings £0.00" *above* "No accounts yet" — a zero before context; the 7-field Add Account modal is a steep first ask; forecast tabs are greyed until 2 statements exist with no "here's what you'll get" preview.
- **The partner importing shared data (project persona, PRODUCT.md 9–11):** the passphrase bar names no sender and no file contents; wrong passphrase = bare red line; two "Import" buttons invite importing a plain JSON backup where the encrypted bundle was meant.
- **Sam (a11y):** `.tx`/`.mi` are `div onclick` — tx-edit and account-menu unreachable by keyboard/screen reader; low-opacity labels on the purple gradient borderline for AA.
- **Casey (mobile):** strong overall (safe-area, 44px targets, 16px inputs); watch the `nowrap` toast overflowing on long "N imported, M skipped" messages.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the LISA card is meant to feel *special*, does one loud purple surface help — or make the calmest tool in the category look like the app it defined itself against? What does "special but quiet" look like (a bolder number, not a bolder colour)?
2. The passphrase is the entire trust model, yet its UI is the least-designed surface in the app. Should the import/sync flow be the *most* crafted screen, not a bare fixed bar?
3. Two import paths and two allowance styles suggest feature-by-feature accretion. Rebuilt from "one number, then a list, then density behind a tap," which screens survive unchanged?
4. For the zero-context stranger, the app is useless until they hand-enter a balance. Is there a 20-second "see it work with sample data" path that keeps the privacy-first promise?
